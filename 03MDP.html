4. Markov Decision Process + Convex Optimization Demo

Back to Contents
This simple applet models investment portfolio management as a Markov Decision Process. In this problem, at the beginning of each trading period (1 quarter), the manager rebalances the portfolio based on their knowledge of the previous quarter's market condition (bear, normal, bull), selecting a strategy \(\mathbf{w} = (w_{stocks}, w_{bonds}, w_{cash})^T\), where \(w_i\) is the weight of the asset type in the portfolio. A risk-adjusted return function based on the Markowitz Portfolio Theory is used to compute the Bellman equation. The optimal strategy is computed as a quadratic programming (QP) problem and solved using primal-dual interior point method.
For each market condition \(s\), the return rates of the assets are given by a vector of random variables \[ \mathbf{r}_s = \begin{pmatrix}r_{\text{stocks}} \\ r_{\text{bonds}} \\ r_{\text{cash}}\end{pmatrix} \] with mean return rate and covariance matrix \[ \begin{gather} \mu_s = \begin{pmatrix} \bar{r}_{\text{stocks}} \\ \bar{r}_{\text{bonds}} \\ \bar{r}_{\text{cash}} \end{pmatrix}\\ \Sigma_s = \begin{pmatrix} \sigma_{\text{stocks}, \text{stocks}} & \sigma_{\text{stocks}, \text{bonds}} & \sigma_{\text{stocks}, \text{cash}} \\ \sigma_{\text{bonds}, \text{stocks}} & \sigma_{\text{bonds}, \text{bonds}} & \sigma_{\text{bonds}, \text{cash}}\\ \sigma_{\text{cash}, \text{stocks}} & \sigma_{\text{cash}, \text{bonds}} & \sigma_{\text{cash}, \text{cash}} \end{pmatrix} \end{gather} \] The raw return (capital gain) of a portfolio \(\mathbf{w}\) under market condition \(s\) is defined as \[ g(\mathbf{w},s) = \mathbf{w}^T \mathbf{r}_s \] where \(\mathbf{r}_s\) is return vector of the coming quarter. Since the manager cannot predict the future and only makes decisions at the beginning of each trading period based on the knowledge of the previous market condition, the risk-adjusted gain of a portfolio \(\mathbf{w}\) under previous market condition \(s\) and risk-aversion parameter \(\lambda\) is defined as (Notation: \(\mathbb{E}^s[\cdot]\) denotes conditional expectation on \(s_{\text{prev}} = s\), \(s'\) denotes the next state after \(s\)) \[ \begin{aligned} g_{\text{adj}}(\mathbf{w},s) & = \mathbb{E}^s[g(\mathbf{w},s')] - \lambda \cdot \mathrm{Var}^s(g(\mathbf{w},s'))\\ & = \mathbf{w}^T\bar{\mu}_{s} - \lambda\cdot \bigg(\mathbb{E}^s[\mathrm{Var} (g(\mathbf{w},s'))] + \mathrm{Var}^s(\mathbb{E}[g(\mathbf{w},s')])\bigg)\\ & = \mathbf{w}^T\bar{\mu}_{s} - \lambda\cdot \bigg(\mathbf{w}^T\bar{\Sigma}_{s}\mathbf{w} + \sum_{s'}P(s'|s)(\mathbb{E}[g(\mathbf{w},s')] - \mathbb{E}^s[\mathbb{E}[g(\mathbf{w},s')]])^2\bigg)\\ & = \mathbf{w}^T\bar{\mu}_{s} - \lambda\cdot \bigg(\mathbf{w}^T\bar{\Sigma}_{s}\mathbf{w} + \sum_{s'}P(s'|s)(\mathbf{w}^T \mu_{s'} - \mathbf{w}^T \bar{\mu}_{s} )^2\bigg)\\ & = \mathbf{w}^T\bar{\mu}_{s} - \lambda\cdot \bigg(\mathbf{w}^T\bar{\Sigma}_{s}\mathbf{w} + \mathbf{w}^T\bar{\Sigma}_{s\to s'}\mathbf{w} \bigg) \end{aligned} \] where \[ \begin{gather} \bar{\mu}_{s} \equiv \sum_{s'}P(s'|s)\mu_{s'}\\ \bar{\Sigma}_{s} \equiv \sum_{s'}P(s'|s)\Sigma_{s'}\\ \bar{\Sigma}_{s\to s'} \equiv \sum_{s'}P(s'|s)(\mu_{s'} - \bar{\mu}_{s})(\mu_{s'} - \bar{\mu}_{s})^T \end{gather} \] The value function and the Bellman equation are \[ \begin{gather} V(s) = \max_{\mathbf{\{w_t\}}}\;\sum_{t = 0}^{\infty} \gamma^{t}\cdot\mathbb{E}^{s_0 = s} [g_{\text{adj}}(\mathbf{w_t},s_t)]\\ V(s) = \max_{\mathbf{w}}\; \bigg\{g_{\text{adj}}(\mathbf{w},s) + \gamma\cdot \mathbb{E}^{s}\big[ V(s')\big]\bigg\} \end{gather} \] where \(\gamma \equiv e^{-(\text{risk free rate})\cdot\Delta t}\) is the discount factor. Because the term \(\gamma\cdot\mathbb{E}^{s}\big[ V(s')\big] = \gamma\cdot \sum_{s'}P(s'|s) V(s')\) does not depend on \(\mathbf{w}\), this becomes a quadratic programming (QP) problem (Note: when \(\lambda = 0\), it becomes a linear programming (LP) problem): \[ \begin{aligned} \max_{\mathbf{w}} \;\; \mathbf{w}^T\bar{\mu}_{s} - \lambda\cdot & \bigg(\mathbf{w}^T\bar{\Sigma}_{s}\mathbf{w} + \mathbf{w}^T\bar{\Sigma}_{s\to s'}\mathbf{w} \bigg)\\ \mathrm{s.t.}\;\;\;\;\;\; w_i & \geq 0\\ \sum_i w_i & = 1 \end{aligned} \] The QP/LP can be solved using the primal-dual interior point method. Note that the objective function and the constraints are all convex. The maximization problem is equivalent to \[ \begin{aligned} \min_{x} \;\; \frac{1}{2}x^T Q x & + c^T x\\ \mathrm{s.t.}\;\;\;\;\;\; Ax & \geq b\\ Gx & = h \end{aligned} \] where \[ \begin{gather} x = \mathbf{w}\\ Q = 2\lambda \cdot (\bar{\Sigma}_{s} + \bar{\Sigma}_{s\to s'})\\ c = -\bar{\mu}_{s}\\ A = I,\; \;b = \begin{pmatrix}0, ..., 0\end{pmatrix}^T\\ G = \begin{pmatrix}1, ..., 1\end{pmatrix},\;\; h = 1 \end{gather} \] Then the Lagrangian is \[ L(x,y,z,s) = \frac{1}{2}x^T Q x + c^T x - y^T(Ax-s-b) - z^T(Gx-h) \] where \(y\), \(z\) are Lagrange multipliers, and \(s>0\) is the slack variable to turn inequality constraints into equality constraints. The optimality conditions (necessary and sufficient) are \[ \begin{gather} Qx + c - A^T y - G^T z = 0\\ Ax - s - b = 0\\ Gx - h = 0\\ y \geq 0\\ s \geq 0\\ YSe = 0 \end{gather} \] where \(Y\) is the diagonal matrix where the diagonal elements are \(y_i\), \(S\) is the diagonal matrix where the diagonal elements are \(s_i\), and \(e = \begin{pmatrix}1,...,1\end{pmatrix}^T\). The first three equations are from the method of Lagrange multipliers to find critical points (\(\frac{\partial L}{\partial x} = 0\), \(\frac{\partial L}{\partial y} = 0\), \(\frac{\partial L}{\partial z} = 0\)). The other three equations are feasibility and slack variable conditions (at the optimal solution, either \(y_i = 0\) and \(s_i > 0\) for inactive constraints, or \(s_i = 0\) and \(y_i>0\) for active constraints). The primal-dual gap is defined as \(\eta = y^Ts\). Geometrically, when \(\eta = 0\), the level set of the objective function and the constraint curves are touching. An interior point of the feasible set satisfies \[ YSe = \delta e \] for some \(\delta > 0\), and the solution to the problem with interior points converges to the true solution as \(\delta \to 0\). This ensures \(y\) and \(s\) are both strictly greater than \(0\). The optimality conditions then become a linear system with 4 equations and 4 unknowns: \[ \begin{aligned} Qx + c - A^T y - G^T z & = 0\\ Ax - s - b & = 0\\ Gx - h & = 0\\ YSe & = \delta e \end{aligned} \] This can be solved using Newton's method:
1. Start with an initial guess \((x^{(0)},y^{(0)},z^{(0)}, s^{(0)})\). For example, \(x^{0} = 0\), \(y^{0} = 1\), \(z^{0} = 0\), \(s^{0} = 1\). Note that in the initial guess \(y^{0}\), \(s^{0}\) should be strictly positive, but the constraints (\(Ax\geq b\), \(Gx= h\)) are not necessarily satisfied.
2. At the \(k\)-th iteration, first calculate the (surrogate) primal-dual gap \[ \begin{gather} \eta^{(k)} = (y^{(k)})^T s^{(k)}\\ \delta^{(k)} =q \cdot \frac{\eta^{(k)}}{m} \end{gather} \] where \(m\) is the number of original inequality constraints (\(Ax\geq b\)), and \(q>0\) is a parameter to be chosen (for example \(q = 0.1\) or \(q = 0.5\)).
3. Calculate \((\Delta x, \Delta y, \Delta z, \Delta s)\) from \((x^{(k)},y^{(k)},z^{(k)}, s^{(k)})\) by solving \[ \underbrace{\begin{pmatrix} Q & -A^T & -G^T & 0\\ A & 0 & 0 & -I\\ G & 0 & 0 & 0\\ 0 & S^{(k)} & 0 & Y^{(k)} \end{pmatrix}}_{\text{Jacobian}} \begin{pmatrix} \Delta x\\ \Delta y\\ \Delta z\\ \Delta s \end{pmatrix} = - \begin{pmatrix} Qx^{(k)} + c - A^T y^{(k)} - G^T z^{(k)}\\ Ax^{(k)} - s^{(k)} - b\\ Gx^{(k)} - h\\ Y^{(k)}S^{(k)}e-\delta^{(k)} e \end{pmatrix} \] 4. Instead of updating \((x^{(k+1)},y^{(k+1)},z^{(k+1)}, s^{(k+1)}) = (x^{(k)},y^{(k)},z^{(k)}, s^{(k)}) + (\Delta x, \Delta y, \Delta z, \Delta s)\), first check the maximum increment so that \(y^{(k+1)}\), \(s^{(k+1)}\) stays strictly positive: \[ \alpha_{\text{max}} = \min \bigg\{ 1, \; \min \{-\frac{y_i^{(k)}}{\Delta y_i} : \Delta y_i < 0\}, \; \min \{-\frac{s_i^{(k)}}{\Delta s_i} : \Delta s_i < 0\}\bigg\} \] If \(\Delta y_i \geq 0\), \(\Delta s_i \geq 0\) for all \(i\), set \(\min \big\{-\frac{y_i^{(k)}}{\Delta y_i} : \Delta y_i < 0\big\} = \infty\), \(\min \big\{-\frac{s_i^{(k)}}{\Delta s_i} : \Delta s_i < 0\big\} = \infty\).
5. Update the state (multiply \(\alpha_{\text{max}}\) by \(0.99\) to make sure \(y\) and \(s\) stay positive): \[ \begin{aligned} x^{(k+1)} & = x^{(k)} + 0.99\alpha_{\text{max}}\Delta x\\ y^{(k+1)} & = y^{(k)} + 0.99\alpha_{\text{max}}\Delta y\\ z^{(k+1)} & = z^{(k)} + 0.99\alpha_{\text{max}}\Delta z\\ s^{(k+1)} & = s^{(k)} + 0.99\alpha_{\text{max}}\Delta s \end{aligned} \] 6. Repeat until \[ \begin{gather} \left\|Qx^{(k)} + c - A^T y^{(k)} - G^T z^{(k)}\right\|_2 < \epsilon\\ \left\|Ax^{(k)} - s^{(k)} - b\right\|_2 < \epsilon\\ \left\|Gx^{(k)} - h\right\|_2 < \epsilon\\ \eta^{(k)} < \epsilon \end{gather} \] The method of Lagrange multipliers only finds stationary points. However, because the objective function is convex (\(Q \succcurlyeq 0\), or when \(\lambda = 0\) the function is linear), the stationary points are minima.
